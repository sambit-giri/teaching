What it computes

Expansion rate

The Friedmann equation gives the Hubble parameter $H(a) = H_0 E(a)$, with scale factor $a = 1/(1+z)$: $$E(a)^2 = \Omega_{r,0}\,a^{-4} + \Omega_{m,0}\,a^{-3} + \Omega_{k,0}\,a^{-2} + \Omega_{\rm DE,0}\,\frac{\varepsilon_{\rm DE}(a)}{\varepsilon_{\rm DE,0}}$$

  • Radiation (photons and neutrinos): $\Omega_{r,0} h^2 = 4.16 \times 10^{-5}$, with $h = H_0 / (100$ km/s/Mpc$)$; zero when "Include radiation" is off.
  • Curvature: $\Omega_{k,0} = 1 - \Omega_0$. With "Flat" ticked, $\Omega_{\rm DE,0} = 1 - \Omega_{m,0} - \Omega_{r,0}$ is filled in.
  • Cosmological constant Λ ($w = -1$): the dark-energy density is constant, and $\Omega_{\rm DE,0}$ is the usual $\Omega_{\Lambda,0}$.
  • Dynamical dark energy, $w(a) = w_0 + w_a(1-a)$: the density changes as $\varepsilon_{\rm DE}(a)/\varepsilon_{\rm DE,0} = a^{-3(1 + w_0 + w_a)}\, e^{-3 w_a (1-a)}$.

Times and distances

Integrals over $a$, done numerically in your browser, with $a_e = 1/(1+z)$ for light emitted at redshift $z$:

Age at $a$$\displaystyle t(a) = \int_0^{a} \frac{da'}{a' H(a')}$

$S_\kappa(r)$ is the transverse comoving distance:

  • flat ($\Omega_{k,0} = 0$): $S_\kappa(r) = r$

  • closed ($\Omega_{k,0} < 0$, $\kappa = +1$): the same with $|\Omega_{k,0}|$ and $\sin$

Plots

  • Scale factor $a(t)$, with today and your $z$ marked. A universe without a Big Bang (a bounce in the past) has no age, so its time axis counts from today.
  • Energy content: each term of $E(a)^2$ divided by $E(a)^2$, e.g. $\Omega_m(z) = \Omega_{m,0}\,a^{-3}/E(a)^2$. The fractions add up to 1 at every $z$ and show when radiation, matter and dark energy dominate.
  • Distances and times against $z$, with your $z$ as a dashed line.

Checking

Compare with Ned Wright's calculator or astropy.cosmology; small differences come from how radiation is treated.

Preset universes

The measured sets are best fits for a flat universe, from Planck 2018 (Planck Collaboration et al. 2020), DESI DR2 BAO with CMB and DES 5-year supernovae (DESI Collaboration et al. 2025), Planck 2015 (Planck Collaboration et al. 2016), Planck 2013 (Planck Collaboration et al. 2014), WMAP 9-year (Hinshaw et al. 2013), WMAP 7-year (Komatsu et al. 2011) and WMAP 5-year (Komatsu et al. 2009). The calculator keeps only $H_0$, $\Omega_{m,0}$, $w_0$ and $w_a$ from them (and treats neutrinos as massless radiation), so its numbers can differ from the papers' in the last digit.

The model universes are the textbook cases: matter only, empty, $\Lambda$ only, and two curved universes, one that recollapses and one without a Big Bang.

Links to a given universe

The values you type are kept in the address bar, so a link opens the calculator with them. Keys: H0, Om, OL (for $\Omega_{\rm DE,0}$), z, w0, wa, flat and rad (0 or 1); left out means the default. For example ?H0=70&Om=0.3&OL=0&flat=0&z=2.
