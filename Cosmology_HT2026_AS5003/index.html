Cosmology

Lecturer: Sambit Kumar Giri

Schedule, homework and announcements are on Canvas.

Book: B. Ryden, Introduction to Cosmology, 2nd ed.

Lectures

  • Lecture 1 Introduction and the fundamental observations Course information, a short history of cosmology, and the introduction to fundamental observations: the dark night sky, homogeneity and isotropy, the CMB, light elements and Hubble's law. Slides PDF
  • Lecture 2 Measuring distances and velocities; cosmic expansion Redshift, the Hubble–Lemaître law, H₀ from supernovae and the Hubble time. Slides PDF
  • Lecture 3 Curvature of space and the Robertson–Walker metric Metrics, special and general relativity, curved space, the Robertson–Walker metric and proper distance. Slides PDF
  • Lecture 4 Distances and the Friedmann equations The horizon distance, Einstein's field equation, the Friedmann, fluid and acceleration equations, and equations of state. Slides PDF
  • Lecture 5 Single-component universes How the energy density of matter, radiation and Λ changes with the scale factor, and the empty, matter-only, radiation-only and Λ-only universes. Slides PDF
  • Lecture 6 Multiple-component universes The general solution of the Friedmann equation, matter with curvature and Λ, radiation and matter, and the Benchmark Model. Slides PDF
  • Lecture 7 Measuring cosmological parameters The deceleration parameter, luminosity and angular-diameter distances, standard candles, and type Ia supernovae and the accelerating universe. Slides PDF
  • Lecture 8 Cosmic dynamics: review The key results of chapters 3–6 in one place, all the distances side by side, and questions to check your understanding. Slides PDF
